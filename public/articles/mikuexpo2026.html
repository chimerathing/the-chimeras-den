VOCALOID SAVED MY LIFE + MY MIKU EXPO 2026 EXPERIENCE


I planned to write about this genuinely life-changing experience as soon as I got home from the venue this past Thursday but I was genuinely exhausted for good few days afterwards so I've kinda just been putting it off. I figured I'd give myself some time to fully gather all my thoughts regarding the event and just how much it meant to me anyways. Better late than never, right?


I've been a vocaloid/vocalsynth in general superfan since I was very very young. Somewhere along the line during the 3rd grade when I was becoming pretty obsessed with anime and turning into an obnoxious weaboo child, I was also simultaneously becoming obssessed with vocalsynths and all the different songs and fan content that the community had to offer. It was my entire life and personality for a good few years, was probably the first time in my life at that point that I had listened to music religiously. I think it's safe to say that it was already destined to be a lifelong special interest. I would frequently talk about it with my family and peers to the point of annoyance and I'd be mocked and called "cringe" simply just for expressing my interest in vsynths. No matter how cringe the people around me thought it was though, my love for vsynths prevailed.


As a child, my appreciation for vocal synths primarily was from a fandom lense. Vsynth characters have always kinda just been blank slates for people to project anything that comes to mind onto, and I heavily enjoyed exploring different interpretations of these product mascots through the stories other people would tell with them. I still enjoy this to this very day, of course, but my appreciation for the music, software, and artists themselves rather than JUST the mascots made to give a face to a synthesized voice, has grown much deeper as I've gotten older and developed a much deeper appreciation for music as a whole. As of now, vocalsynths have become much more than just characters or software to me. To me, they're a symbol of human creativity, inspiration, and a reminder that everyone is capable of making something incredible if they really try. I love Hatsune Miku and I love all the people responsible for her having songs to sing in the first place, as they're the reason I continue to make art and strive to become better at what I do, so that one day I can also inspire people. One of these days I'll learn how to compose my own music and expand my skillset further.


2024 was an extremely dark time in my life. I hadn't processed the fact that a relationship I had just gotten out of was incredibly abusive and I was desperately yearning to feel loved by my abuser again, my home evironment was just as shit as it always had been except worse with my family being outwwardly transphobic to me again, and homeschool was making me want to rip every single last strand of my hair out. Overall, I was having difficulty finding really anything to in life to enjoy and make me feel like it was worth living again. At this point in my life, my obsession with vocalsynths had died down quite a bit for a good few years since I actively went out of my way to expand my taste in music beyond the realm of vocalsynths. Nothing in the vocalsynth scene had particularly caught my eye in a long time anyway. Kasane Teto's SynthV voicebank had released in the midst of My Big Murder Drones Hyperfixation of 2023, that was pretty cool, but I had bigger things to worry about! Such as arguing with miserable people in the murder drones fandom over dumb shit that never actually mattered. Was I was one of those miserable people myself? Oh for sure, I'm not a saint, in fact I was actively infecting the people around me with my misery. Being a source-attached fictive/introject of a character with a popular ship that you despise (or once despised) is NOT for the weak! I'm getting pretty off-topic though.


Sometime in 2024 while the stress and depression stemming from everything I described above was hitting me like 30 massive trucks repeatedly running me over and crushing every bone and organ in my body and I was in a constant debate with myself as to whether I wanted to die or keep living in hopes of things eventually getting better, I came across the song "Machine Love" by Jamie Paige, of course, featuring Kasane Teto. Teto already was one of my favorite vsynth characters aside from Miku since my interest in the medium began as a wee little boy, but listening to this song permanently cemented her as my definitive favorite ever, hence why the website you are currently looking at is almost entirely themed around her. Hearing this song also completely reignited the love I thought I had lost for vocaloid and vocalsynths long ago, and sent me down a rabbit hole of discovering a whole world of amazing newer vsynth songs for me to enjoy, as well as rediscovering old ones that I loved so much as a child. Most of the "vocalsynth music" I had listened to for a few years before this point after my interest went dormant was utaite covers of certain vsynth songs, and I instantly became unable to stand listening to any utaite cover because it didn't hit as hard as hearing a robot sing as if it had a soul. Jamie Paige is definitely my all-time favorite voca-producer, and I don't care how newgen that sounds to you because I've been a fan of vsynths since 2017 and I even listened to JamieP before she got a boom in popularlity due to her usage of Teto so Fuck You. Real ones remember Love Taste


It was thanks to this sequel to my childhood obsession with vocalsynths that I gained a newfound appreciation for life in the form of human connection driven by art, and I also engaged with the community by learning how to create my own covers, primarily using SynthV, which heightened my appreciation for the art form and producers even further. Because of this, I was especially fucking ecstatic to be able to attend Miku Expo for the first time ever in the big year of 2026. I've always wanted to go to a Miku concert or literally just any vsynth-adjacent event since I was little, so the moment I saw there were tickets available for the concert in New Motherfucking Jersey (the state I live in btw I didnt know we had motion like that), it was an oppurtunity of a lifetime that I absolutely could NOT pass up. The 2024 expo flew under my radar due to my aforementioned life and mental health issues that made it hard to care about anything, so I was not gonna miss my chance to go this time around. I probably wouldn't have enjoyed going in 2024 anyways due to the endless amount of complaints that I heard regarding how shittily the event was organized and the lack of effort to properly intergrate the replacement of the infamous hologram setup with an LED screen into the scenery onstage.


I understand people's gripes with the LED screen completely, the hologram gave a wonderful illusion of Miku and the other cryptonloids actually physically being there on stage, so to have that suddenly replaced with the characters being trapped in a rectangle void with the whole rest of the stage fully illuminated after years of anticipating the return of in-person concerts after the COVID-19 pandemic definitely had to be incredibly disappointing. In 2025 they replaced the small LED screen with a bigger one which opened the door for songs that had only ever been peformed in Japan at Magical Mirai now being able to be performed worldwide at Miku Expo, and they also made more of an effort to blend the screen in with the rest of the stage, which alleviated some frustration about the screen destroying the illusion that the hologram gave. I was confident that they would REALLY step up their game for the 2026 expo due to other vocaloid concerts that have happened since 2025 then being much more visually interesting overall, and oh fucking BOY I was right. It was genuinely incredible. They added backgrounds behind the characters in a handful of songs so the characters werent just dancing in a dark void, and while the visuals weren't anything like crazy insane you can tell that they genuinely took the time and care to make this concert way more memorable. The visuals for CONNECT:COMMUNE (ily flavor foley) in particular were my favorite and I genuinely got kinda emotional both seeing and hearing it live. Miku genuinely looked so real there and like if it was possible my dumb gay ass would've run up to the stage to hug her. I'm normal I swear.


I just wanna say also the setlist for this year was fucking incredible?? Like genuinely the best one they've had in my opinion. Majority of the songs that played were favorites of mine (except TYQOON that one was such a nothingburger. They could've picked ANY other song as a Meiko solo.) and they brought out a couple of the really good classics too and it was great. I was singing along and screaming my head off the whole time and just in general I had so much fucking fun. Without a doubt this was the best experience of my life and I hope to go again in 2028 when they come back to North America. The one and only thing I'm upset about is that they didn't play Kimagure Mercy at the venue I went to. Setlist C would've been absolute fucking perfection they just had to replace Rasberry Monster with Kimagure Mercy. Come on. If you got Setlist A at your location fuck you (lightheartedly). I'm also bummed that I didn't get to go to Digital Stars in NYC the day after the main concert cuz I really really really reallllly wanted to see Flavor Foley live, but what's done is done I suppose. It's funny because by the time it was even confirmed that Flavor Foley was gonna be in NYC it was already too late for me to get tickets for Digital Stars LMFAO fuck my life man. I don't care if YOU got to hear Kimagure Mercy live at Miku Expo and Flavor Foley live at Digital Stars it should've been ME instead. (i do care i'm really happy for you but i'm jealous regardless). I got to hear Amazing Dolce, Magical Cure Love Shot and CONNECT:COMMUNE live though so I can still die a happy man now anyways.


All that being said, I do have a few things to say regarding how the event was run specifically at the location I went to. My mom bought general admission tickets for the two of us, she's been going through a lot of financial struggles recently so she couldn't afford to get VIP tickets, and by the time she even HAD the money to by tickets the only seats that were left were general admission ones, so I'm just thankful that we even got to go at all. When we arrived, I explained to her that there would be a line for the people who purchased VIP tickets to be let into the venue early, and that general admission would be lined up to go inside later. She went ahead and got on the line regardless even though we obviously didn't have VIP tickets, but there were other people on the line saying that the front of the line was for VIP while the back of the line was for general admission??? Which confused the shit out of me, of course. We stood on line for a minute before getting off so my mom could call the event staff to figure out What The Hell Was Happening And Where We Were Supposed To Go, and they said the same thing so we just got back on the line. We stood there for like over 30 minutes before someone came over and told all the general admission people to get off the line because it was for VIP only. Ridiculous. So we all got off the line and proceeded to all stand around in a giant cluster of people IN THE ASS FREEZING COLD for like an entire hour. At some point some bitchass motherfucker blew a goddamn whistle and the entire crowd went running for the entrance doors thinking that was the signal to go inside, and the guy laughed as we all got backed up by security. It was funny as fuck though honestly I can't blame him we're all just standing out there impatient as fuck and then suddenly we all go collectively running like a herde of cattle. Insane work. Of course I went running myself and my mom had to grab onto me so that I wouldn't get trampled LMFAO. Eventually though they just had us all shuffle through eachother to get inside rather than actually lining any of us up which was stupid as hell but whatever.


Actually trying to get into the venue was anxiety inducing. We had to walk fucking 300 million miles across the building to get to our seats and each second I spent walking a could feel my legs aching more and more and my bones literally felt like they were going to shatter. But eventually we got to our seats which were REEEALLY high up and the high-up seats in Prudential Center are fucking steep as hell with the narrowest possible walkways so I had to lean onto the seat handles while getting to my seat because I was so fucking scared that I would fall. Our seats also just ever so convieniently happened to be RIGHT NEXT TO THE FUCKING STAIRS so for like basically the entire concert there would periodically be people walking by and blocking my view of the stage and thus also getting in the way of the video I was taking of the whole concert to share with my friends. Our seats were on the right-hand side of the venue so we were looking at the stage at an angle. Worst possible seats we could've gotten but that's what happens when you're broke and have to wait last minute to be able to afford going to fun events. Regardless of all that though I still genuinely had a blast and I enjoyed singing my heart out, waving around my cheap unofficial lightstick, screaming "MIKU MIKU BEAM" during Magical Cure, and shouting "I LOVE YOU FLAVOR FOLEY" at the top of my lungs when CONNECT:COMMUNE came on. My voice was completely strained afterwards but that's the beauty of it. Genuinely priceless moment in time. I felt like I was gonna have a heart attack the whole time because of how loud that place was but that's just the price to pay when enjoying live music. It was worth it to see Miku in person for the first time ever, and also be surrounded by so many people who adore the medium just as much as I do, so I'm not complaining. Thank god I brought earplugs! Also why was literally everyone and their fucking mother at the Newark expo like HUH. Jamie Paige was there and I never even saw her. I didn't know New Jersey had motion like that!


I got a handful of cute free souveniers from people and also I brought my 3DS with me and ended up getting streetpassed by like 3 people. Genuinely an unforgettable experience and I'm really excited to have another chance to go in the coming years. Moral of the story life is worth living because the act of creation is beautiful and the act of coming together to enjoy something that others have created as a collective is even more beautiful. I wish I could've cosplayed at the event like I planned to or actually gotten a chance to properly interact with other people there, but regardless, I'm still so grateful to have had the priviledge to be there for something that has brought so many vsynth fans across the globe together.
